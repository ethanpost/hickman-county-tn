## Call to Order
- The budget committee meeting was called to order after informal microphone checks and background conversation.
- Roll call recorded 9 present and 5 absent, which was stated to be enough for a quorum.

## Approval of Agenda
- A motion was made and seconded to approve the agenda.
- The agenda was approved by voice vote with no opposition noted in the transcript.

## Public Comment
- One public commenter spoke primarily about the school budget.
- The commenter said they had reviewed school budget figures since 2018, adjusted for inflation, and looked at per-student comparisons.
- Concerns raised included the number of assistants, growth in health services costs, special education budget increases, technology and Chromebook costs, and the absence of capital expenditures from the school budget.
- The commenter said school capital expenditures had averaged about $700,000 per year and said roughly $700,000 in needs had already been identified.
- The commenter argued against increasing maintenance of effort while student population is falling.
- The commenter cautioned against comparing Hickman County's school funding share to counties with different revenue bases, citing counties with roughly $39 million budgets and as much as $7 million in local sales tax collections.

## 32nd Judicial District Child Advocacy Center
### Presentation
- The district attorney described the importance of the Child Advocacy Center for Hickman, Lewis, and Perry counties, especially for forensic interviews in child abuse cases.
- The presenter said forensic interviews can preserve testimony and reduce trauma for children in criminal cases.
- One example involved a defendant who agreed to a 40-year prison sentence at age 62 after evidence was developed from a forensic interview.
- The forensic interview in that example was described as 2.5 hours long and detailed enough to support a search warrant and recovery of evidence.

### Funding Request
- The Child Advocacy Center requested $30,000 from the county, the same amount requested the previous year.
- Last year's request was for salary support for the forensic interviewer and executive director positions.
- This year's request was intended to help reimplement therapy services in the 32nd Judicial District by the end of the year.
- If grant funding covers therapy, the presenter said community funding could support a Child Protective Investigative Team coordinator position focused on case coordination, prevention, and outreach.
- The center also plans to pursue future opioid abatement grant funding, United Way of Hickman County support, VOCA funding for the family advocate, and Department of Children's Services funding for the executive director and forensic interviewer.

### Discussion and Vote
- Commissioners discussed how other counties contributed, with Lewis County described as giving about $10,000 last year after a $15,000 request and Perry County described as giving $15,000.
- The requested Hickman County contribution was discussed as part of the nonprofit budget line, increasing a line that already included about $23,000 for other nonprofits to about $53,000.
- A motion to approve $30,000 for the Child Advocacy Center passed by roll call vote, 12 yes and 0 no.
- Commissioners later reopened the nonprofit budget and formally added the $30,000; that follow-up motion also passed 12 yes and 0 no.

## School Fund 141
### School System Overview
- The incoming director of schools presented the school budget and described positive academic trends.
- The presenter said several schools moved from D ratings to B ratings, multiple schools reached level 5 in TVAAS growth, and Hickman County High School became a reward school for the first time in its history.
- The school system highlighted dual enrollment opportunities allowing students to graduate with up to a full year of college credit at free or low tuition.
- The CTE program was described as having about 15 programs, which the presenter said is unusually broad compared with many high schools.
- ACT scores were said to have increased over the last four years, attendance rates were said to be higher at almost every school, and teacher retention was described as good.

### Budget Pressures
- The school system said ESSER pandemic relief funding has ended after previously paying for positions, facility upgrades, and technology.
- Enrollment has declined, with current enrollment reported at 2,824 students and the prior-year figure used for TISA revenue reported at 2,893.
- About 800 Hickman County students were described as choosing private school or homeschool.
- The presenter compared that to statewide figures of about 13% homeschooled and 10% private school, saying Hickman County's combined figure was also about 23%.
- Homeschool and private school participation was described as being more concentrated in K-8 grades.

### Revenue and Efficiency Plans
- The school system plans to promote its virtual school option, which is currently approved for in-county students and at the time served grades 6-12.
- The presenter said the school system is looking at expanding the virtual option to K-12.
- Possible revenue sources discussed included advertising on about 40 school buses and allowing communication towers on school property.
- The school system also plans to seek grants, reduce non-essential spending, review non-critical positions, and try internally to spend only 90% of the approved budget where possible.

### Property Tax Request and Salary Mandate
- The school system requested an 8-cent property tax increase tied to the state teacher salary mandate.
- The minimum teacher salary must increase to $50,000 for the coming school year.
- Additional mandated salary minimums were stated as $50,645 for years 1-5, $53,370 for years 6-10, and $56,900 beginning with year 11.
- The school system estimated the mandate would cost about $700,000, possibly slightly more.
- Commissioners discussed the current zero-year teacher salary schedule amount of $47,924 and described moving to $50,000 as about a 4% increase.

### Capital Maintenance Request
- The school system separately requested help with capital maintenance projects totaling a little over $400,000.
- The presenter said the original capital project list had been over $700,000 and had been narrowed to the most urgent items.
- Tennis court resurfacing was discussed, including a reported $80,000 figure for high school tennis courts and clarification that the request involved both East Hickman High School and Hickman County High School.
- Commissioners discussed whether such projects should be handled through a capital outlay note, adequate facilities funds, or possibly a wheel tax.

### Local Option Sales Tax Discussion
- Commissioners discussed whether the local option sales tax estimate should remain at $2.9 million or be increased.
- The actual 2024-2025 local option sales tax figure was described as $3.2 million, and some commissioners expected the next figure to be higher, possibly around $3.4 million, $3.5 million, or $3.6 million.
- School representatives said the school system receives 50% of what the county receives regardless of the budgeted number, and that increasing the budgeted number would affect maintenance of effort.
- A motion was made to reject the school budget and send it back with guidance to increase the local option sales tax estimate to $3.3 million and reduce the property tax request to about 4 cents.
- The motion was described as reducing the property tax increase to about $335,874.
- The motion to reject the proposed Fund 141 budget with guidance passed 12 yes and 0 no.

## School Fund 143: Central Cafeteria
- The school system said it will continue the free lunch program next year.
- Expenditures were lower partly because summer expenditures budgeted the prior year were not needed going forward.
- Participation in breakfast and lunch programs had increased in recent months.
- Revenue estimates were based on current reimbursement levels and recent participation, without assuming a future reimbursement increase.
- A slight staffing reduction of about one position was noted.
- A motion to approve Fund 143 passed 12 yes and 0 no.

## School Budget Follow-Up Schedule
- Commissioners discussed the need for the school system to return after the Fund 141 budget was sent back.
- The school system was asked to return at the next Monday meeting, expected around 5:30 p.m., depending on other scheduled business.

## Solid Waste
### Budget Drivers
- The solid waste presentation restated the major reasons for needing more revenue.
- Drivers included higher per-ton landfill costs in Camden, loss of about one-third of paid business from Perry County, aging equipment, engineering costs for a permit, and the need to prepare for another 10-year solid waste plan due in fiscal year 2028.
- The presenter said the permit process can take about six years, so engineering work needs to begin early.

### Collections
- Commissioners asked whether collections had improved for people who had not paid solid waste fees.
- The presenter said about 90.5% of current-year accounts had either paid or claimed an exemption.
- The presenter said about $60,000 in prior-year debt had been collected over the prior two months.
- Court action was mentioned for June 30 to pursue some unpaid accounts.

### Proposed Fee Resolutions
- Commissioners discussed two pending solid waste resolutions that were under review by the county attorney.
- One resolution would increase the residential fee from $90 to $110 and increase the tipping fee from $60 to $100.
- A new commercial and industrial charge of $120 per year was also discussed.
- The commercial and industrial tipping fee was also described as increasing from $60 to $100.
- No action was taken on the solid waste budget during this meeting; commissioners expected to revisit the resolutions at the fourth Monday county commission meeting in May.

## Reopening Fund 101 and Nonprofits
- Commissioners reopened Fund 101 and the nonprofit budget to make budget adjustments.
- The nonprofit budget was adjusted to add the $30,000 for the 32nd Judicial District Child Advocacy Center.
- Fund 101 also needed tax-rate adjustments and a broadband grant adjustment.
- The broadband grant was described as budget neutral because it involved $100,000 in both revenue and expenditures.
- After adjustments, the projected ending fund balance for June 30, 2027 was described as just under $4 million.
- A motion to approve Fund 101 with the adjustments passed by roll call vote, with all recorded votes in favor.

## Announcements and Scheduling
- Commissioners noted that solid waste resolutions would be voted on at the fourth Monday commission meeting in May.
- A budget meeting was also planned for the following Monday.
- The budget schedule would need to be revised because of the changes made during the meeting.

## Adjournment
- With no other business noted, a motion to adjourn was made and seconded.
- The meeting adjourned by voice vote.
