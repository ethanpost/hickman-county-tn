## Agenda Items

### Hickman County Attractions Proposal for ACT Pavilion Management
- A proposal was presented for Hickman County Attractions to manage the ACT Pavilion while combining the National Banana Pudding Festival and Agon Arts Foundation under one umbrella.
- The stated mission was to organize volunteers to produce events that attract tourists to Hickman County and improve opportunities and quality of life for citizens.
- The presentation emphasized that the pavilion and related events should target regional and national tourism rather than only local attendees.

### National Banana Pudding Festival Performance
- The 2025 National Banana Pudding Festival was described as a record event with 105 vendors and close to 350 volunteers over both festival days.
- The festival served 8,000 pounds of pudding, including about 3,700 containers served by nonprofit organizations and 4,650 containers sold on the pudding path.
- Attendance was reported at 9,343, short of a 10,000-person goal.
- A random zip-code sampling had 650 responses, representing 45 Tennessee counties, 36 states plus the District of Columbia, 298 U.S. counties, and six countries.
- The presenter said this demonstrated the festival's reach beyond Hickman County and Tennessee.

### Visitor Data and Tourism Standards
- Placer AI data from 2023 estimated 8,300 visitors to the festival.
- Visitor travel distances included ranges of 20 to 30 miles, 50 miles, and 100 miles or greater.
- Visitor profiles showed disposable income at 118 compared with 100 as the national average, and average income 12 basis points above the national average.
- The average age was described as slightly below the national median age of 38.38.
- Households with children were reported 10 basis points above average, and visitors with a college degree or greater were reported 24 basis points above average.
- The presenter said Tennessee tourism standards classify tourism events as drawing significant crowds from beyond 50 miles or greater.

### Local Spending and Revenue Leakage
- Visitor traffic data showed 1.1% of festival visitors went to Dunkin' Donuts in Fairview before the festival, while 1.5% went to Fish Camp afterward.
- The presenter argued that the ACT Pavilion's location in Centerville helps pull visitors across the county and capture spending at local gas stations, restaurants, hotels, and Airbnbs.
- The relocated rodeo at Cowboy Church was described as successful, but the presenter said much of the gas, hotel, and restaurant tax revenue likely went to Dickson County.
- The county's lack of hotel capacity was described as a "chicken and egg" problem, with events needed to support future hotel recruitment.

### Agritourism and Rural Event Model
- The presenter described agritourism as a leading tourism focus in Tennessee and said Hickman County is positioned to benefit from that market.
- The ACT Pavilion was described as a potential stop or resource for the Agon Arts Tour and other farm or cultural heritage events.
- The Lynchburg / Jack Daniel's model was used as an example of a major tourism draw located on two-lane roads in a small rural community.
- Lynchburg was described as having about 700 residents, with 750 people working at the Jack Daniel's Distillery and more than 300,000 visitors each year.
- A Jack Daniel's race was cited as having 1,774 runners and a participation cap below 2,000 people.

### Requested Hotel / Motel Tax Allocation
- Hickman County Attractions requested 40% of the hotel/motel tax, described as the remaining 40%, to help recruit or contract with a facility manager and event coordinator.
- The presenter said the 40% would not fully fund the position.
- A proposed compensation model included a monthly rate plus commission, such as 10% or 5% of profits from successful events.
- The presenter gave an example that if an event brought in $100,000, the coordinator could earn an additional $10,000.
- The original request was for a five-year term, with the presenter saying three years might be needed to develop the position and five years would avoid aligning the decision with an election cycle.

### Fundraising and Community Benefit
- The Banana Pudding Festival was reported to have given back $87,000 the previous year.
- The presenter said the festival is a 100% nonprofit fundraiser, with proceeds going back to the community.
- The same fundraising model could potentially be used at the ACT Pavilion through parking, concessions, and other event-related revenue.

### Potential Events at the ACT Pavilion
- Potential event ideas included concerts, multiple-stage music events across Hickman County, agritourism events, rodeos, stock shows, youth events, car shows, and other festivals.
- A person connected to the festival board was described as having Live Nation or sound-industry experience and connections with country music performers.
- Vendor demand for the Banana Pudding Festival was described as high, with vendor spots sold out by mid-April after being open for 45 days.
- A removable flooring option was discussed as a way to support events such as car shows and reduce dust issues in the arena.
- Flooring that would normally cost about $100,000 to $150,000 was reportedly sold at auction for $8,000 the previous month.

### Parking and Insurance Issues
- The relocated rodeo was said to have moved because of a parking issue.
- The parking issue was described as an insurance liability matter involving a neighboring property owner's land.
- Discussions were underway with the property owner about insurance and possibly leasing land for event parking, though the owner was not yet committed.

### Rodeo and Arena Capacity Discussion
- Members discussed whether the rodeo could expand into multiple rodeos, timed events, barrel racing, competitive bull fighting, or jackpot-style events.
- The ACT Pavilion layout was expected to allow seating for close to 2,000 people using existing bleachers, folding chairs, and rented bleachers.
- Because the rodeo moved, bleacher seating was limited to about 1,200 people, leaving many people standing.
- The Cowboy Church arena was described as a 120-by-120 square, which limited team roping; two rope-and-belt-buckle prizes reportedly remained because no qualifying runs were completed.
- Attendance at the rodeo was discussed as roughly 2,000 people.
- A similar round-robin team roping event in Alabama was cited as drawing about 2,000 spectators and running nearly 20 hours each day for two days.

### Committee Direction on ACT Pavilion Proposal
- Members discussed the proposal but did not appear ready to make a motion or vote that night.
- The presentation materials had been emailed to members for review.
- The presenter was thanked, and the committee indicated the discussion was not over.
