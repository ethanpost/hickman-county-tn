### County funding — amounts and comparisons (as stated in testimony)

- **Last year’s county donation:** **$156,000** for countywide fire protection, divided among **five** departments.
- **Population basis:** about **26,000** citizens cited for per-capita math.
- **Implied prior per-resident county share:** about **$6 per citizen** for fire protection (**$156,000 ÷ ~26,000**).
- **Comparative benchmarks cited by the speaker:** about **$16 per citizen** as a low-end volunteer-fire funding figure seen elsewhere nationally; up to about **$70 per citizen** in other volunteer contexts; **well over $200 per citizen** at minimum if the county had a **full-time paid** fire department.
- **This year’s spoken “minimum” ask:** **$475,000**, framed as about **$18 per citizen** at the same population (~26,000) and as the “must haves” before service is compromised.
- **Written request on commissioners’ paperwork:** a commissioner said the packet figure was **“6338”** in the transcript — in context almost certainly **~$633,000** (e.g., six hundred thirty-three thousand); the chief confirmed the higher written total and described **$475,000** as the reduced “bare minimum” ask. **Verify against the actual packet** if the exact written request matters.
- **Per-capita framing:** speaker said **$18 per citizen** is still at the **low end** of national volunteer comparisons and roughly **$180 less per citizen** than full-time paid service cost (as phrased in the meeting); rhetorical line tied to that figure: **“What’s a life worth? 18 bucks a year.”**
- **Equipment / operations examples:** about **$20,000** in gear to onboard **one** volunteer firefighter (no wage); **diesel** cited at **$6 per gallon** that day; hypothetical federal grant of **$1 million** with **$100,000** local cost-share mentioned to illustrate grant matching.
- **Grant odds:** speaker cited roughly **20–30%** chance of receiving certain gear-related grants (percentage, not dollars).
- **Apparatus:** replacing a truck could take on the order of **$1.5 million** (“million and a half dollars”) per the speaker — not a formal quote.
