## New Business — EMS Station 3

### Current Operations

- EMS Station 3 is open **12 hours per day** on **12-hour shifts**; director has staffed it for now.
- Two paths discussed for full use of the building:
  - Hire additional staff for ongoing **12-hour** coverage, or
  - Install a **sprinkler system** and move an ambulance crew in for **24-hour** operation.

### Fire Marshal and Plan Review History

- Early in the project, a letter from the fire marshal's office stated **no review** was required for the building plans submitted.
- Original plans showed a **personal workspace**, not sleeping quarters; architect submitted plans to the state fire marshal's office.
- A later inspector visit raised occupancy/sleeping-quarters issues; rooms **102 and 103** were on plans as offices but were functioning as sleeping quarters.
- Plans submitted **April 16** requested no review for renovation of an existing building.
- Eric Blstead served as general manager of the construction project; Mr. How also oversaw work.
- Building was **not formally shut down** by the fire marshal; EMS leadership chose to vacate and operate from another station after the inspection concerns.
- Inspection report read aloud did **not** list stove, hood, or sprinkler deficiencies—only that revised plans for sleeping quarters must meet applicable codes.
- Commissioners expressed concern the fire marshal may move requirements again after a sprinkler is installed; they want **written confirmation** that a sprinkler system alone would make the building compliant for **24/7** ambulance operation.

### Sprinkler System — Cost and Feasibility

- Preliminary sprinkler estimate from Eric Blstead: **up to $70,000** (described as high-end; variables remain).
- Final building cost was **$1.2 million**, right on the approved amount.
- Approximately **$21,000** remained in the building appropriation; after a **~$12,000** fence, roughly **$9,000** would be left—leaving an estimated **~$50,000** short at the high sprinkler estimate.
- Bon Utility capacity check (Brad Barnes) found water pressure and flow adequate to sprinkle without a separate tank—helping keep cost toward the lower end of the estimate.
- Sprinkler water entry must remain accessible from outside for fire department hookup; a front office room may serve that access point.
- Sprinkler design, fire marshal approval, and installation estimated at roughly **three months** before **24-hour** occupancy could begin.
- Sprinkler work likely cannot finish before the current budget adjustment funding the temporary **12-hour crew** runs out (after **June**).
- No sprinkler companies contacted yet; chair and commissioners asked the mayor to pursue estimates immediately, with goal of numbers before the budget committee meeting (~one week) and possible action at full commission later in the month.
- Matthew Barnhill offered to help the mayor with sprinkler company contacts.

### Staffing Alternative — 12-Hour Crew Costs

- To staff Station 3 **seven days a week, 12 hours per day** without moving a crew from the east station: **four additional staff**.
- Jesse's high-end figure: **$275,000 per year** without benefits; with benefits, roughly **$100,000** more (~**$375,000** total, as discussed).
- Current workaround: both crews remain at the east station while a **12-hour crew** rotates into Station 3 daily.
- Alternative if not hiring: move one crew from the east station to Station 3 (**24 hours**) after sprinklers are approved.
- The temporary **12-hour crew** is **not** funded from the original year budget; money was reallocated. FEMA funds and borrowed money (with credit to Valerie Woodall and Crystals) replenished sheriff and EMS budgets after an "energetics" event.
- Continuing the **12-hour crew** past **July 1** would require further budget action by the commission.
