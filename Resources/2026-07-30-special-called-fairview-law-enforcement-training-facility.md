## Official Presentations

### Planning Commission Timeline — Commissioner Claude Callicott
- Early 2025: then Planning & Zoning Director Robert Axton reported Fairview Police Department and the Hickman County Sheriff’s Office had approached him about a training facility
- Matter placed on the **April 2025** Planning Commission agenda (open meeting with public comment)
- Deputy Cox and a Fairview Police Department representative presented plans; property had not yet been purchased
- Facility described as **private**, not open to the public; site in **A1 zoning**
- Planning discussed that private training facilities / gun ranges are allowed in A1 under the zoning ordinance and that appearance before the commission was largely a courtesy
- **January 2026** Planning Commission meeting: property had been purchased; site plan presented and **approved** (open meeting with public comment)
- Callicott turned discussion over to the Sheriff

### Sheriff’s Office — Sheriff Jason Craft
- Fairview contacted the Sheriff’s Office about **a year and a half ago** about building a training facility with a firing range after difficulty finding a location in Williamson County
- Properties considered in Hickman County included one on Bates Trail
- Sheriff’s Office introduced Fairview to codes and the Planning Commission
- Fairview indicated willingness to sign an MOU / interlocal agreement; **none has been signed yet**; mutual aid with surrounding agencies described as typically verbal
- Examples of mutual aid: AES event (Fairview east side, Centerville west side); ice storm assistance from another county’s sheriff
- Addressed rumors: **no military training**; **no explosives** at the facility (Fairview indicated they have no explosives)
- Fairview described as intending to be good neighbors; Hickman County personnel welcome to use the facility and attend trainings **at no cost**; training schedule to be shared when available
- Most training expected to be **classroom**; range use less frequent
- Hickman County typically trains on a firing range about **four times a year** (**four days**); in-service usually Monday–Friday with classroom first four days and range on Friday
- Constant five-days-a-week shooting described as not financially feasible given ammunition costs

### County Attorney — Dan Mecklenborg
- Reviewed the record; site plan reviewed at the **January 6** Planning Commission meeting
- Then building commissioner Austin Brown indicated the use was permissible in **A1** as a governmental facility
- Planning Commission voted **unanimously** to approve the site plan
- In **July**, a requested appeal to the Board of Zoning Appeals was brought forward (transcript names “Mr. Whims” as associated with the request)
- Mecklenborg told planning staff (Brooke) to hold the appeal pending analysis
- Request included review by an independent attorney; Timothy Potter (Dickson firm) retained for that analysis

### Outside Counsel Opinion — Timothy Potter (Reynolds Potter Reagan and Dad, Dickson)
- Reviewed Planning Commission minutes, especially **January 6, 2026** site-plan approval, and citizen filings seeking to halt / appeal the project
- Legal focus: whether process was followed and whether an appeal was timely
- Opinion: under ordinance and Tennessee statute, there are **60 days** to appeal a governmental action (including to Board of Zoning Appeals regarding a building commissioner’s zoning interpretation)
- About **six to seven months** passed after the unanimous public-hearing action before formal contest
- Cited a **2014** vesting-rights statute; opinion that the property owner now has a **vested interest** in the approved site plan
- Advice: county Planning Commission and Board of Zoning Appeals **lack legal authority to halt the project**; letter to that effect to be sent to the two known complainants, with copies to county officials
- Opinion addresses process/timeliness, **not** the merits of the project
