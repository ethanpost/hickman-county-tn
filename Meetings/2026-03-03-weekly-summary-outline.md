## 3/2/26 Health Safety and Properties Committee Meeting

### EMS Report and Staffing Concerns
- **Budget status**: Overtime and part‑time lines stressed by winter storm and AES closure but expected to finish the year within budget if no new major events occur.
- **Staffing and retention**: EMS director reports loss of multiple paramedics to higher‑paying agencies; currently has open paramedic positions with no applicants; some staff cannot afford to live in the county on current pay.
- **Role of advanced EMTs**: State has expanded AEMT scope (cardiac drugs, EKG transmission, pain meds), increasing responsibility without commensurate pay.
- **Commission discussion**: Members emphasize the seriousness of EMS funding and staffing, especially with an aging population, and urge that pay and retention be addressed in the upcoming budget cycle.

### EMA Winter Storm Costs, Grants, and Generators
- **Storm operations recap**: EMA director reviews preparation beginning January 19 and ongoing response/recovery since January 24 winter storm.
- **Cost overview**: Approximately $157,000 in storm costs identified so far (contractor strike team, rental equipment, materials), not including overtime and fuel for multiple departments.
- **Public Assistance (PA)**: Volunteer labor (roughly 4,100 hours from five fire departments and Team Rubicon) is being used as match toward the federal PA grant; EMA expects most storm costs to be reimbursable if documentation is completed correctly.
- **Individual Assistance (IA)**: County has met the per‑capita damage threshold; federal decision on IA approval is still pending.
- **Generator grant**: EMA has started a mitigation grant process for three natural gas–fed generators (EMS Station 1, City Fire Station 1, EMS Station 2) with an estimated total cost of ~$120,000; with a 12% match split between city and county, the county’s share is projected at about $7,200.
- **Commission comments**: Officials publicly thank EMA, volunteer fire departments, Team Rubicon, and citizens for extensive storm response and community support.

### Sheriff’s Department Report and Camera System Needs
- **Aging camera systems**: Courthouse and jail camera systems are about 20 years old, have short retention windows (5 days at courthouse, 3 weeks at jail, 14 days at sheriff’s office), and sometimes skip several seconds of footage or fail to capture critical incidents.
- **Liability examples**: Past events include an alleged use‑of‑force complaint and an inmate jumping from an upper level; in both cases, limited or failing cameras complicated investigations and created litigation risk.
- **Upgrade proposal**: Vendor quote of roughly $190,000 to unify and modernize courthouse and jail cameras, extend retention to around six months, allow remote access via tower operators, and provide capacity for roughly two dozen additional cameras for future jail expansion.
- **Funding approach**: Sheriff notes potential to use savings from restructured inmate medical costs and emphasizes long‑term legal and safety benefits; no immediate vote is requested.

### Nuisance Property Ordinance (Resolution 2609)
- **Purpose and scope**: Resolution would give the sheriff’s office tools, grounded in Tennessee Code, to address chronic problem properties (e.g., drug houses, frequent disorderly conduct) and nuisance businesses.
- **Mechanism**: After repeated calls, sheriff’s office could work with county attorney to bring property owners—not just renters—before a judge, compelling cleanup or tenant changes and placing responsibility and potential penalties on owners.
- **Examples**: Sheriff cites a residence that generated roughly 60 calls in six months (drugs, fights, disorderly behavior) as a situation this ordinance is designed to address.
- **Exemptions and questions**: Discussion clarifies that the ordinance explicitly excludes organizations such as the Girl Scouts and is not aimed at legitimate community groups; commissioners ask how renters vs. landlords will be handled.
- **Status**: No vote taken; members will continue reviewing text prior to consideration at a later meeting.

### Roadway Solicitation and Door‑to‑Door Permitting (Resolution 2611)
- **Motivation**: Recent complaints involve people collecting money at busy intersections and going door‑to‑door for high‑pressure sales or scams (e.g., tree cutting, driveway paving, storm repair) without clear ties to local causes.
- **Key provisions**: Proposed resolution would:
  - Require permits through the sheriff’s office for intersection roadblocks/collections and commercial door‑to‑door solicitation.
  - Allow dispatch and deputies to verify whether solicitors are permitted and remove unpermitted groups when necessary.
- **Exemptions and refinements**: Girl Scouts and school‑approved fundraisers are intended to be exempt; county attorney will refine language and formatting into standard Hickman County resolution style.
- **Commissioner concerns**: Members share past experiences with “travelers” and fraudulent fundraisers and view the permitting system as a public‑safety and consumer‑protection measure; fee structures may later be considered to help fund security upgrades such as cameras.

### Future Jail and Justice Center Revenue Resolutions
- **Smokeless tobacco commissary fund**: Sheriff proposes a resolution to place profits (not costs) from smokeless tobacco sales in the jail commissary into a dedicated fund for jail and justice center improvements (e.g., isolation cells, fixtures, courtroom seating, security upgrades).
- **Compliance and cost control**: Program is described as self‑funded and a behavior‑management tool—since purchases must go through inmate accounts, it has reduced frivolous medical visits, intentional plumbing damage, and negative account balances; hospital and medical costs are down compared to prior years.
- **Revenue expectations**: Initial projections suggest roughly $80,000 profit in the first year and potentially up to $200,000 over two years, with funds available for capital needs like the proposed camera system; taxpayers would not bear these specific costs.
- **Asset sale proceeds**: A separate forthcoming resolution would allow the sheriff’s office to retain proceeds from the sale of donated or surplus equipment (e.g., trucks and trailers obtained at no cost), using those funds for vehicles and equipment rather than returning them to the general fund.

### Hickman County Attractions and Ag Pavilion / Banana Pudding Festival Proposal
- **Management concept**: Representatives from Hickman County Attractions (HCA) outline a proposal for a five‑year management agreement under which HCA would manage day‑to‑day operations and bookings at the Ag Pavilion while the county retains ownership.
- **Organizational structure**: HCA is now a 501(c)(3) parent organization under which the National Banana Pudding Festival (a 501(c)(4)) and the Ag & Arts Foundation would affiliate, enabling unified tourism planning and access to more grants.
- **Hotel‑motel tax request**: Proposal asks that the remaining 40% of hotel‑motel tax (currently often used for recreation, ballfields, and some pavilion projects) be allocated to HCA to help fund a contracted full‑time manager ($40–$45k/year), with the rest of compensation tied to event revenue.
- **Planned benefits**: Manager would focus on facility upkeep, scheduling, event coordination (festival, rodeo, Ag & Arts events, additional agriculture‑related events), grant writing, and expanding use of underutilized assets like the concession stand; local nonprofits and teams could earn money through parking and concessions.
- **Economic impact discussion**: Supporters highlight that the Banana Pudding Festival donates tens of thousands of dollars annually to nonprofits and significantly boosts local sales tax; critics worry about diverting funds from youth recreation, past grants to nonprofits whose missions some commissioners question, and the risk of investing heavily in a challenging facility.
- **Next steps**: No vote is taken; commissioners emphasize the need for due diligence, careful review of the business model, and possible adjustments before any final decision, with expectation that the matter will return in future meetings.

### Land Use Plan Amendments
- **Concern about fiscal risk**: Commissioner presents proposed amendments to the county land use plan to ensure it is not interpreted as obligating existing taxpayers to fund major water, sewer, or road expansions for growth.
- **Key wording changes**: Suggestions include:
  - Reframing “expansion of infrastructure” language to emphasize fiscally responsible, selective expansion that does not increase tax burdens on current property owners.
  - Replacing directives to “expand” water/sewer coverage with language that “encourages” development where infrastructure is funded by developers or beneficiaries.
  - Adding bullets to condition rezonings, density increases, and large‑scale residential approvals on proven infrastructure capacity or developer‑funded improvements.
