## Public Comment

- **First speaker:** Spoke in support of removing the 5-acre rule wording from the growth plan and keeping the 1-acre rule. Cited Tennessee law (growth plan cannot be changed for three years once approved). Raised concerns about affordability (e.g., 10 acres appraised over $200,000 vs. one-acre lots around $30,000), cost of maintaining five acres, impact on parents giving land to children, and an estimated 40% property tax revenue loss. Noted property tax is the majority of county budget revenue (over $10.5 million for FY 24–25). Asked if there is a plan to recoup lost revenue without raising rates. Suggested a “means and way” committee for funding issues. Stated zoning has already limited affordable housing (e.g., single-wides banned); referenced state law that zoning must promote health, safety, morals, convenience, order, prosperity, and welfare. Asked the body to vote to remove the 5-acre verbiage per the open motion.
- **Second speaker:** Clarified the proposal is 3–15 acres, not 5, and that most citizens are against it. Concerned that removing all acreage numbers from the land use plan could allow planning and zoning to set any minimum (e.g., 20 acres) so long as it “aligns” with the plan. Stated anything above 3 acres is not a solution for this county (unlike high-growth areas). Recommended at most 3 acres (e.g., one to three for A1, A2, A3). Echoed concerns about landowner impact and tax base.
